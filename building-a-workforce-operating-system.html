Case Study 4

Building a Workforce
Operating System

Workforce Strategy  •  Business Operations  •  Process Transformation

Oracle Health was standing up a new Product & Engineering organization with executive priorities and ambitious growth plans, but almost none of the operating infrastructure needed to manage hiring at that scale. I was tasked with creating the hiring process and budget tracker.

2,500+Hiring requests managed
$30M+Resource budget
~12,000Person organization at launch

01   The Starting Point

The challenge was not fixing a broken process. There was no process.

The organization was new. Executives had growth priorities and approval authority. Finance had budgets. Talent Acquisition had recruiting work underway. Hiring managers had roles to fill.

What was missing was the operating layer that connected those pieces and turned an approved hiring need into something the organization could manage, track, and make decisions against.

I was given the objective to build a hiring process and budget tracker, then figure out what the system needed to be.
Executives

Set priorities, approved growth, and made tradeoff decisions.

Finance

Maintained budgets and financial records.

Talent Acquisition

Owned candidate outreach, screening, interviews, and recruiting activity.

Hiring Managers

Needed a clear way to request roles and understand how hiring worked.

The Missing Layer

A shared operating model connecting requests, approvals, budget, recruiting activity, expected starts, and leadership decisions.

02   What I Built

I designed the hiring operating model from the ground up.

I defined how requests entered the system, what information managers had to provide, how budget and approval status were captured, how requests moved through the process, what rules governed decisions, and what leaders needed to see in recurring reporting.

Once an executive had approved the business need, the request entered the operating system I built.

Talent Acquisition owned the recruiting process itself. My role was to connect that work to the larger operating picture.

I brought together hiring requests, approvals, budget position, recruiting activity, pipeline status, and expected starts so upper leadership could see where each request stood, what was moving, and where decisions or tradeoffs were needed.

I also worked with Finance to build the budget and capacity logic behind the process, including approved headcount, salary versus budget, RSU spend, geographic cost differences, time to hire, recruiting pipeline, and the financial tradeoffs involved in hiring decisions.

03   Hiring Economics

The work was not just tracking open roles. It was helping leaders understand the tradeoffs behind them.

Approved hiring value depended on factors such as role, location, and level, so a hiring decision was not simply a matter of counting open positions.

Managers needed to understand how those variables affected available budget and the choices they could make within it. I built those rules into the operating process and partnered with Finance to make the financial impact visible.

04   Manager Onboarding

Building the system was only half the work.

Managers also needed to understand how to use it and how hiring worked inside Oracle. I onboarded managers across the organization to the new process, including how to submit and manage requests and how role, location, and level affected assigned hiring value.

The operating model had to work for the people using it, not just for the people reporting on it.

That meant translating enterprise hiring rules into a process managers could follow consistently, while keeping Finance, Talent Acquisition, and upper leadership aligned around the same portfolio.

05   How the Model Scaled

01

Build What Was Needed

There was no operating model to inherit. Within the first few weeks, I built a working process using approval documents, a dedicated inbox, spreadsheets, and defined decision rules.

It was intentionally practical. The organization needed something that worked immediately.

02

Add Structure

As the organization began using the process, I strengthened it around what we were learning.

That included clearer intake requirements, approval tracking, budget controls, recurring reporting, and manager onboarding.

03

Automate for Scale

Eventually, volume made the manual model unsustainable.

Even after I brought in an additional program manager to assist, the number of requests, budget variables, handoffs, and status changes created too much opportunity for human error. I translated the operating model into requirements for an Oracle APEX application.

04

Mature the System

The goal was never to preserve the system just because we had built it.

As Oracle's broader operating infrastructure matured, a more robust APEX-based model developed within Oracle Cloud Infrastructure became available, and the organization ultimately moved to that enterprise solution.

Build what was needed. Learn from how it was used. Automate when the manual process reached its limits. Move to the stronger enterprise solution when one became available.
